Glossary

MCU (microcontroller)

A microcontroller is a small, self-contained computer on a single chip. It has a processor, memory, and input/output pins all built in, and it runs one program over and over to read sensors and control hardware like motors, lights, and servos.

On an ROV: the MCU is usually the part that turns commands from the surface into motor signals. It reads the joystick input, sends throttle signals to each thruster's ESC, and reports back sensor readings like depth, heading, or leak detection.

MCU vs. single-board computer

An MCU (like an Arduino, Raspberry Pi Pico, or STM32) has no operating system. It starts your program instantly and reacts with precise timing, which is ideal for driving motors. A single-board computer (like a Raspberry Pi 4/5) runs Linux and can handle video and networking, but its timing is less predictable. Many ROVs use both: the computer handles cameras and communication, and the MCU handles the hardware.
