Pass inspection the first time

Safety inspection is pass/fail, and you get only three attempts — fail all three and you're out of the water for good. More teams fail on wiring workmanship than on anything clever. Here's the checklist judges actually use.

⚠ Three strikes

Fail three safety inspections and you're disqualified from the underwater portion — no appeals. You can still compete in the engineering & communication events, but the pool is closed to you. Rehearse the checklist like you rehearse the mission.

Electrical & fusing

  • One blade fuse (ATO or MINI, 32 VDC, color-coded) in the positive line, within 30 cm of the Anderson Powerpole connector. Nothing — no switch, no E-stop — sits between the connector and the fuse.
  • Size the fuse from measured Full Load Amps in water — submerge the ROV, run all thrusters full, read the ammeter, pick the next standard size up. Do not add the old 150% margin.
  • Red/black Anderson Powerpoles at the single power connection — roll-crimped (a hand "squish" crimper fails), with the two halves locked together.
  • Bring your own spare fuses. You get exactly one replacement mid-run; a second blown fuse ends your run. MATE supplies none.
  • No onboard batteries on the ROV — all power comes down the tether. (Only the separate float may carry a battery.)
  • Power disconnect kills everything in under 5 seconds.
  • Never exceed 12 V nominal anywhere (the MATE supply may reach 14.8 V — you can't step it up).

Wiring & workmanship

2026 is an explicit crackdown year on wiring discipline and strain relief. This is where teams bleed points.

  • Strain relief on BOTH ends of the tether (ROV side and topside). Tape, glue, and zip ties are explicitly not acceptable — use a real strain-relief connector (e.g. a Kellems grip). The ROV should hang from its tether without stressing the wires.
  • No exposed copper, no bare wire, no exposed motors. Tape alone never counts as a seal — use solder + waterproof sealant + heat-shrink (silicone self-vulcanizing tape is preferred underwater).
  • All motors waterproofed. "Disposable" unwaterproofed motors are banned. Brushless motors count as exposed unless you can document them as sealed.
  • Show a fuse symbol on your wiring diagram (SID) using a proper ANSI/NEMA/IEC symbol, with your measured FLA. RANGER & NAVIGATOR SIDs must be CAD-drawn; SCOUT may hand-draw. Never copy MATE's example.
  • Any AC power physically separated and labeled from DC (or state clearly that you use none).

Mechanical

  • Propellers shrouded. SCOUT: enclosed in the frame or shrouded. NAVIGATOR/RANGER: full IP-20 — the shroud encircles the prop, extends ≥2 mm front and back, mesh <12.5 mm, and a ½" dowel or finger cannot touch the blade from any angle. Gears need the same (new for 2026).
  • No sharp edges, no glass anywhere on the ROV or deck — this includes glass-faced monitors, which are banned outright. Flush-cut every zip tie.
  • Hand-launchable — no cranes, winches, ladders, or bracing as part of launch/recovery (new for 2026).
  • Enclosures rated for depth (up to 5 m at Worlds).
  • Close-toed shoes on deck; safety glasses when working on the vehicle.

Fluid power & lasers

  • SCOUT: manual pumps only, water or air, no pressure accumulators; any pressurized container needs a ≥¼" vent so it never exceeds ambient pressure.
  • NAVIGATOR / RANGER powered fluid power: pass the Fluid Power Quiz at 100% by April 30, 2026 (no exceptions). Hydraulic (water) ≤150 psi; pneumatic (air) ≤40 psi; regulator + shut-off + relief valve required; no SCUBA tanks; accumulators ≤1.25 L.
  • Lasers (NAV/RANGER only): visible red ≤5 mW or green ≤1 mW, Class I/II/IIIa, powered from the surface, with a permanently-attached beam stop. Submit specs to MATE by May 15.

The JSEA RANGER

A Job Safety & Environmental Analysis is a real offshore-industry safety tool, and for RANGER it's now a gate: no JSEA at the mission station means you cannot run the product demo at all. It also earns 5 points just for being presented before setup, plus up to 10 for its quality.

The three-step JSEA structure
  1. Describe the job steps — break deck/dive operations into clear steps.
  2. Identify the hazards at each step.
  3. Assign safeguards — following the hierarchy eliminate → engineer → PPE — and name a person responsible for each.

Scope it to deck and dive operations only (setup, power-up, poolside ops, teardown) — not shop/tool safety. It's a separate document, not folded into your technical report.

Print-and-carry pre-inspection checklist

  • FLA measured in water; correct fuse selected and shown on the SID
  • Fuse within 30 cm of the Powerpole; nothing between
  • Anderson Powerpoles roll-crimped and locked together
  • All props and gears shrouded to IP-20
  • No exposed copper/motors; no glass; no sharp edges
  • Every splice soldered + sealed + heat-shrunk (not tape alone)
  • Real strain relief on both tether ends
  • AC separated & labeled (or "no AC used")
  • Fluid Power Quiz passed (if using powered fluid power)
  • JSEA printed, scoped to deck/dive ops, in hand (RANGER)
  • Float has its own SID and documentation (if built)
  • Spare fuses in the kit
