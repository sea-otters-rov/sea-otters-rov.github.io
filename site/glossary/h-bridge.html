Glossary

H-bridge

A circuit of four switches that lets one power supply run a DC motor forward or backward.

A brushed DC motor reverses when you swap its two wires. An H-bridge does the swap electronically. The motor sits in the middle of an "H" with a switch in each leg. Close one diagonal pair and current flows through the motor one way; close the other pair and it flows the opposite way.

On an ROV

Every brushed motor that must run both ways, like a bilge-pump thruster or a claw motor, needs an H-bridge. You rarely build one: a brushed ESC or a motor-driver board already contains it, and your MCU just tells it direction and speed.

Common confusion

Never close both switches on the same side (S1 + S2, or S3 + S4). That shorts the supply straight to ground, which is called shoot-through. Do this, and you might let the magic smoke out!
