Glossary

MCU

Short for microcontroller: a tiny computer on one chip that reads sensors and drives motors.

A microcontroller has a processor, memory, and input/output pins all built into a single chip. It runs one program over and over, reading inputs and controlling hardware like motors, lights, and servos.

On an ROV

The MCU usually turns commands from the surface into motor signals. It reads the joystick input, sends throttle signals to each thruster's ESC, and reports back sensor readings like depth, heading, or leak detection.

MCU vs. single-board computer

An MCU (like an Arduino, Raspberry Pi Pico, or ESP32) has no operating system. It starts your program instantly and reacts with precise timing, which is ideal for driving motors. A single-board computer (like a Raspberry Pi 4 or 5) runs Linux and can handle video and networking, but its timing is less predictable. Many ROVs use both: the computer handles cameras and communication, and the MCU handles the hardware.
