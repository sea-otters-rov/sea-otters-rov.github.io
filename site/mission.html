The storyline

MATE's 24th season is tied to the UN Decade of Ocean Science and the brand-new Decade of Action for Cryospheric Sciences — the global push to understand Earth's frozen regions. Companies travel to St. John's, the oldest city in North America, home to two extraordinary research facilities:

World's largest

The Flume Tank

1.7 million liters, currents 0–1 m/s, a 20 m × 3 m viewing window. At Worlds, some tasks make you hold position in a real current.

Down to −25 °C

The Ice Tank

90 m × 12 m × 3 m, grows real ice at 2.5 mm/hour. The float task literally happens under the ice.

The real science woven through the tasks: a newly-found cold-water coral garden off Funk Island; invasive European green crabs; icebergs threatening four real oil platforms (Hibernia, Hebron, Terra Nova, Sea Rose); Equinor's real Hywind Tampen floating wind farm; and NSF's fleet of 500 robotic ocean-profiling floats.

Task 1 Seabed 2030 — a coral garden in cold water

A dense cold-water coral garden was found improbably shallow off Newfoundland. Your job: sample it and map it.

  • Collect specimens — grab basket stars (O-balls) and coral (chenille on PVC). Full credit for collecting coral by the substrate.
  • Model the garden — measure its length and height, then either photogrammetry (autonomous, hardest, most points), a CAD 3D model, or a hand-drawn 3-view technical drawing.
  • Fly a transect — at regionals, fly end-to-end over the garden; at Worlds, hold position in a current for 30 seconds.
Gripper design cue

The coral and basket-star bases are ½" PVC targets. Design your claw around gripping a ½"-diameter pipe and you cover several tasks at once.

Task 2 SmartAtlantic — the big one

The largest task by points. Five sub-missions built from real ocean-monitoring problems:

  • Invasive crabs — recover or count European green crabs (image-recognition scores highest in RANGER), then upload the count.
  • Iceberg tracking — survey the iceberg's perimeter, measure its keel depth, then classify the threat level (Green / Yellow / Red) to four real oil platforms using latitude, longitude, and heading.
  • Whale-safe gear — trigger a simulated acoustic release and recover a "ropeless" lobster pot off the bottom.
  • Recover the buoy anchor — attach your own recovery line to a U-bolt and bring it up.
  • Service the observatory — swap an eDNA sensor and calculate percent-frequency from a data table the judge hands you (a pure data-literacy point).
Two different threat systems — don't mix them up

In RANGER, platform threat is scored by distance (>10 nm Green, 5–10 nm Yellow, <5 nm Red) but subsea-asset threat is scored by keel depth vs. water depth — and that one is all-or-nothing. Practice both with the six worked examples MATE provides.

Task 3 Wind-powered offshore oil platform

Modeled on the real Hywind Tampen floating wind farm. Sequential pick-and-place actions — the most mechanically approachable task, and a great rookie first target.

  • Retrieve a power connector from the wind-farm station.
  • Lay its cable through a waypoint.
  • Install it into the oil-platform port (a Velcro-hook connection).
  • RANGER adds: place a bubble curtain, guide a micropile into a sleeve (without touching it — 5-pt penalty per touch!), and pull a release pin.

Task 4 MATE Floats — build a profiler

A genuinely different challenge: build a small, self-contained vertical profiling float (a non-ROV device) that dives and surfaces on its own, modeled on NSF's real GO-BGC ocean floats.

A rookie point plan

You don't need every task to place well. Stack the reliable points first, then reach:

  1. Task 3 — mechanically simple pick-and-place. Your bread and butter.
  2. Task 4 data graph + float deploy — close to free if you prepare.
  3. The eDNA percent-frequency calculation — a data task, no piloting.
  4. Task 1 specimen collection — once your gripper is dialed in.
  5. Iceberg threat lookup — build a simple decision table and drill it.

And remember: the engineering & communication deliverables are worth as much as the whole pool run. Every hour on your technical report counts double.
