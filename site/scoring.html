Win on paper, too

The design-rationale jackpot

In both the technical documentation and the engineering presentation, "design rationale" — explaining why and how you decided — is the single biggest scoring bucket (40% of the doc, 45% of the presentation). Judges reward a decision matrix, testing data, and honest build-vs-buy reasoning far more than a description of what you built.

We have the receipts

This isn't theory. Our two SCOUT teams finished 5th and 6th in 2026 with a top-3 pool run in the whole region — but a marketing poster scored 20/50. The podium was 12 points away, sitting in text boxes we didn't fill in. See exactly what the judges wrote in Our 2026 Scorecard →

Product demonstration

The pool run: complete themed tasks against the clock. Key rules that quietly cost points:

  • Time bonus — 1 point per full minute + 0.01 per second left, but only if you finish all tasks, surface under your own power, and touch the vehicle before time runs out.
  • Penalties (5 pts each): pulling the tether to recover, requesting diver help (clock keeps running), leaving debris in the pool.
  • You don't have to surface between tasks — plan an efficient route.
  • RANGER: present your JSEA before setup for 5 points, and stay under 25 kg for a weight bonus of up to 10.

Technical documentation

A written report (≤10 pages SCOUT/NAV, ≤25 RANGER) judged on how well you explain your engineering. Point weights:

SectionWeight
Design rationale (the big one)40%
Overall report / formatting15%
Safety10%
Teamwork10%
Critical analysis (testing & troubleshooting)10%
Accounting / budget10%
Wiring diagram (SID)5%
  • Title page, abstract (≤150–250 words), a photo of the finished vehicle, captioned design photos, SI units.
  • Design rationale must show your step-by-step process, trade-offs, a real explanation of buoyancy physics, and explicit build-vs-buy justification.
  • New for 2026: AI may not write your documentation or presentation (you may describe using it as a tool). No copy-paste from past years.
Start it on day one

Keep a project notebook all season — research, trade studies, test data, who did what. "Any problem that takes more than 20 minutes to solve is worth writing down." Writing the report in April is trivial if you logged as you went.

Engineering presentation

  • No slides. PowerPoint/Keynote are banned — it's a face-to-face technical brief with the (unpowered) ROV in the room. Handouts are fine; up to two 30-second silent videos are allowed to show it moving.
  • Scored standalone — anything in your report you don't also say out loud earns nothing here.
  • Every member should genuinely understand the whole vehicle. Judges ask about build-vs-buy, safety philosophy, and how the theme shaped your design — deferring to a specialist teammate is fine.
  • System design / design rationale is ~45% of the score.

Marketing display (poster)

A poster judged by a non-technical panel — treat visitors as future clients who don't know what an ROV is. Worth 50 points (60 in RANGER with the onsite re-score).

  • Design rationale is "the bulk of the poster" (11 pts) — again, the why beats the what.
  • Hard size cap: 36" × 48". Go over and you lose 5 points and forfeit the Best Marketing Display award.
  • Word limits are enforced: an abstract over 150 words costs a point. Proofread for length, not just spelling.
  • Required sections: Abstract, Company Information, Design Rationale, Theme (tie in the UN goals), Company Evaluation, Acknowledgements — and thank MATE by name.

Easy points & hard deadlines

Free-ish points

Grab these

Company Spec Sheet — an "easy 20 points" that's pure one-page checklist compliance. Corporate Responsibility — up to 20 optional points for a single well-documented mentoring session or water-quality project.

Don't get burned

Watch the calendar

Documentation is due as a correctly-named PDF by a hard deadline (RANGER: May 21, 2026, Hawaii time) — no late submissions, no revisions. Your first submission's timestamp is final. Wrong file name or size loses points on the Initial Review.

The rubric's own rule

MATE scores 0 (missing) → 3 (meets: addresses all requirements) → 4 (exceeds: exceptional depth and breadth). To climb from 3 to 4, add analysis and evidence — don't just describe, justify.
