Season Plan 2027

Last year we were great in the pool and lost on paper. This year the plan is simple: keep the pool skills, stop giving away free points, and pay our own way.

Judges also flagged: two teams with near-identical designs, and suspicion that adults built the vehicles. All of this is fixable — and fixing it costs effort, not money.

Season goals

  • If we compete in Scout: podium.
  • If we compete in Navigator: top 5 is a great result.
  • Either way: score at least 80% of the documentation & poster points. That's where the podium was lost.
  • Money: fund the season through sponsors and fundraising — and pay parents back.

Decision #1 — which class?

Recommendation: 1 Navigator + 1–2 Scout

Veterans build the Navigator vehicle; new members build for Scout, with veterans helping them get started. Different classes = genuinely different designs, which also fixes the "identical twins" problem from last year.

Why not Ranger this year

  • The regional allows one Ranger team per school — and that slot most likely belongs to the other team at our school already. (confirm with them)
  • Ranger competes in a 4 m pool. One of our pools might be that deep — measure it. If it really is 4 m, this argument weakens; the others still stand.
  • Budget: $450 registration + a realistic $1,500–2,500 build vs. our proven $650 builds.
  • Ranger documentation is twice the weight (100+100 pts, JSEA, CAD SID) — and documentation was our weakest side even at Scout level.
  • Ranger effectively requires computer vision (crab counting: 15 pts with CV vs. 5 manual). We've never done CV.
  • A more experienced team we know lost in Ranger last year.
  • Navigator already brings every new challenge on our list: required cameras, IP-20 shrouds, CAD SID, an independent profiling float.

The path: a strong Navigator season in 2027 → Ranger in 2028 with budget, experience, and a deep-water practice plan.

The two jobs that matter

Company titles (CEO, CMO, CSO…) get assigned in October when teams form — and MATE does require them. But two real jobs need names now, as acting leads with a review at the end of September:

Documentation Lead

  • Builds templates from the judges' rubrics, week one.
  • Collects the "what we decided and why" notes after every sprint.
  • Guards limits: pages, words, file names, deadlines.
  • Doesn't write everything — subsystem owners write their own sections. That's also Q&A training.
  • If it's too much for one person, the role splits in two: photos/notebook and final documents.

Marketing Lead

  • Owns the poster — and claims every checklist point: abstract, captions, photo credits, sources, thank-yous (including MATE).
  • Runs the fundraiser and the sponsor pitches.
  • Keeps the website and the game demo ready to show.

When teams split in October, each lead trains a counterpart on the other team. Same standards, both teams.

How we work

  • Students' hands only. Adults advise with words; tools stay in student hands. The photo binder is our proof.
  • Every decision gets a written "why" within a week of making it.
  • Two meetings a week, two hats: engineering time and documentation/marketing time both get protected hours.
  • Test, then trust. Anything that took 20+ minutes to figure out gets written down — that's the Critical Analysis section writing itself.
  • Everyone rehearses Q&A for their own subsystem. No frozen silences in front of judges.
